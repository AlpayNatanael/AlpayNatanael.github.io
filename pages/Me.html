About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in mathematics at the University of California, Irvine. My research brings together probability, harmonic analysis, complex analysis, optimization, and machine learning. I work across mathematical theory, algorithm development, and numerical experiments.

I use AI and large language models as conjecture engines: tools for proposing formulas, exploring patterns, and testing possible approaches. I examine their assumptions, search for counterexamples, and use reproducible computations to guide the work. Mathematical conclusions ultimately rest on rigorous proof.

My applied projects include prioritizing drug and vaccine safety reporting signals, complex-valued kernel regression, and mathematical visualization. In ongoing research on portfolio compression and deep hedging, I study how representation and learning errors affect financial risk.

I am interested in collaborations where mathematical reasoning helps make computational methods more reliable and interpretable, with clear assumptions and evidence for their performance.
