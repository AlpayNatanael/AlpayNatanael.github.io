Teaching

My teaching at the University of California, Irvine spans numerical analysis, machine learning, calculus, linear algebra, analysis, differential equations, geometry, optimization, and the history of mathematics.

I have served as instructor of record for Numerical Analysis I and II, and as a teaching assistant for lower- and upper-division mathematics courses.

I also lead directed reading projects that help undergraduates connect mathematical theory with computation and develop experience reading and presenting research.

Directed Reading and Undergraduate Research

I mentor undergraduates through UC Irvine’s Directed Reading Program (DRP), connecting mathematical reading with computational exploration and research presentations.

Deep Hedging

Current DRP

I lead a directed reading course on deep hedging, bringing together probability, optimization, and machine learning in the study of financial risk. We explore the mathematical formulation of hedging and how neural networks can be used to learn trading strategies, with an emphasis on worked examples and computational implementation.

High-Dimensional Probability and Random Matrices

Past DRP

I guided students through the study of random projections and the preservation of Euclidean norms in high dimensions. The project combined readings on subgaussian random variables and random matrices with numerical experiments comparing Gaussian, Bernoulli, uniform, and Rademacher constructions.

Students investigated norm distortion and presented their work in the following posters:

  • Preserving Vector Norms Using Subgaussian Random Matrices Student contributors: Nguyen Tran, Da Min Choi, Alan Li, Hanyun Wang, and Keven Zhuang. Spring 2026 · Poster (PDF)
  • Rademacher Random Matrices as Near-Isometries on the Unit Sphere Student contributors: Aparajita Bandopadhyay, Madeline Chu, and Collin Cheung. Poster (PDF)
